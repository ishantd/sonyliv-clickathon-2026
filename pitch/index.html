01  /  The problem
02 / 14

A session boundary is not
a viewer.

Counting every session between its first and last event is the obvious implementation, and on the unseen day it is wrong by a fifth. Phones get pocketed, players get paused, streams stall — all of it keeps a session technically open and none of it is an audience.

02  /  What the brief asks
03 / 14

Five questions.
Five answers.

1
Defining an active interval when the heartbeat is missing, the player is paused, or the app is backgrounded
A five-clause predicate in a versioned policy.yaml — started ∧ ¬terminated ∧ foreground ∧ playing ∧ live within 120 s
0.2%gaps falsely split
2
Representing active ranges — arrays, intervals, minute deltas, or a hybrid
Hybrid. Normalized intervals are the truth, signed ±1 deltas are the serving form, a dense minute tier sits on top
43×fewer rows than events
3
Minute-wise peak and average without scanning raw session history
Prefix-sum from a day anchor, never t = 0. Average is time-weighted, not sampled at boundaries
5 ms8,192 rows read
4
Staying filter-friendly across platform, country, content, video type, grain
Nine dimension combinations in the sort key. Peak computed after aggregating — never by summing per-dimension peaks
1,100 @ 11:23a slice's own peak minute
5
Handling sessions that are still open and keep growing
A quiet session falls out of a WHERE clause — nothing is written when a viewer stops. History takes signed corrections
37,649never closed. absorbed

04  /  Architecture  II — the read path
05 / 14

Two questions.
Two structures.

stage 02concurrency_deltas
6,435,460 rows  ·  34.9 MiB  ·  nine masks
Fast becauseTwo rows per interval per mask, and the answer is sum(delta) over the full key — merges can lag without changing it.
We didAn explicit column list, because a bare Summing table sums your dimensions. Plus a conservation gate that throws with the ratio.
checkpointbucket_net · day_anchor
1,004,654 + 190,980,048 rows  ·  32.8 MiB
Fast becauseA window read never sums from t = 0. It starts at the nearest anchor, so cost is bounded by the window, not by history.
We didAnchored per service day, so each day is self-contained and a new day loads as an isolated partition.
servingconcurrency_minute_versions
1,295,876 rows  ·  4.3 MiB  ·  dense minutes
Fast becauseNo cumulative sum at read time at all. A whole-day peak and time-weighted average comes back in 5 ms reading 8,192 rows.
We didImmutable generations bound to a policy and a sealed snapshot. Peak computed after aggregating the requested combination.
live, independentsession_live_now
96,844 session states  ·  2.6 MiB
Fast becauseO(open sessions), never history — identical cost on day 1 and day 300. A quiet session falls out of the WHERE clause.
We didNothing is written when a viewer stops, which is the whole hard problem: ClickHouse has no primitive that fires on absence.
One insert-time MV in the whole chain — deltas → bucket_net — kept as a canary: an empty next statement means the MV did not fire SonyLIV × ClickHouse

The challenge What it looked like How we solved it Result
A third of the day never closed 37,649 sessions with no End
25,403 with no Start		 An open session is published with a provisional end at its lease expiry. The next heartbeat re-derives it and the pipeline publishes the difference — +1 at the old expiry, −1 at the new one. 0 rebuildscurve correct as of its watermark
Backgrounding almost never signalled only 6.51% of sessions carry both bg and fg The tuning day had 100% coverage and would have let us lean on bg/fg entirely. The lease was built as the primary exclusion signal instead, with the background event as a hard edge on top of it — not as a replacement. heldno rule depended on bg arriving
Liveness detection dropped Apple silently 58.6% IPHONE
63.6% APPLE_TV
emit no heartbeat trio		 Liveness qualifies on any event rather than the periodic trio. A trio-based rule returns a plausible number with most Apple traffic missing and raises nothing. full coverage21 platforms, none dropped
Pause is invisible at the schema level 42 event names hidden under one event_type The playback axis is parsed from the event column, not event_type. Pause and error stop playback; play and resume restart it; error never terminates. 98,703pause events correctly excluded
A doubled curve passed every check we had exactly 2× the peak, all invariants green Balance checks cannot detect a scalar multiple. It changed the schema, not just the demo

The honest reason concurrency_minute_versions exists is this integration. A flat, dense, one-table read surface is what makes text-to-SQL answerable. An LLM reliably writes max(minute_peak) … WHERE minute_start BETWEEN …. It will not reliably write a day-anchored cumulative sum over millisecond boundaries.
